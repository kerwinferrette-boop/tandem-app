# LLM Council — EPIC-11 / D23 rest-modifier fork

**Date:** 2026-09-17
**Convened by:** scheduled loop session, attended (Kerwin live)
**Question asked:** Should an external training-load signal (EPIC-11, "athlete load awareness") be permitted to modify rest duration — a D23 ownership question?

## Framed question

D23 (ACTIVE, enforced by scripts/doctrine.mjs) states "PHASES is the sole owner of rest, and authoredRest is the only channel by which any deviation reaches the user." EPIC-11's Expected Behavior (Notion) asks that >=4 external training sessions/week "adds +15 sec to all restComp values." Two concrete problems found in the 2026-09-08 audit: (1) no citation exists anywhere for +15 seconds specifically — D23's own cited source (research-report (8).pdf §3 "Training Variables by Goal") prescribes rest PER GOAL, not per external training load, and §6 "Skill Level Progressions" is silent on rest; (2) the rest band is already over budget before adding anything — PHASES.build_muscle's late-phase restComp of 135/150s already exceeds §3's cited 90-120s hypertrophy-compound band, a standing flagged-open item in D23 itself.

Options weighed: (a) permit external-load to add a rest deviation through a new or extended authoredRest-compatible channel; (b) reject outright — external training load may affect volume/cardio prescription but never rest; (c) a hybrid (e.g., a load-aware advisory shown to the user without changing the actual rest value the engine uses).

## Advisor responses

### The Contrarian
The audit is right, and I'd go further than "flag it" — this is two separate red flags stacked on top of each other, and stacking makes it worse, not just twice as bad. First, the citation problem is worse than "no citation for +15 seconds." I pulled §3 directly: rest is prescribed per goal (60–90s upper isolation, 90–120s lower compound, 3–5min strength) — flat numbers, zero mention of external load as a rest modifier. But the report does have a real biometric-adaptation framework (§7, HRV/sleep/VBT readiness states). I read it end to end. Every lever it names for "compromised recovery" — volume cut 20-30%, intensity cut to 70-80%, deload days, frequency change — and rest duration appears nowhere in any of the three readiness states. That's not silence, that's a source that discusses recovery-driven programming in detail and pointedly never reaches for rest as the dial to turn. EPIC-11 isn't an unfilled gap, it's a mechanism the closest analogous research explicitly doesn't use. Second: you're proposing to add a deviation to a value D23 itself flags as already broken (135/150s against a 90-120s cap). Fixing that upstream problem first would change the baseline +15s gets added to. Shipping (a) now means either compounding a known-bad number or building the new feature on a foundation you already know you'll have to rip up. Reject (a) outright. (c) is the only defensible option, and only if it surfaces something the source actually supports — session count as a fatigue signal, advising volume/deload, not rest.

### The First Principles Thinker
What are we actually trying to solve? Not "should external load add 15 seconds to rest" — that's already a dead question, because the number doesn't exist anywhere in the source base. The real question underneath EPIC-11 is: what problem is a user training 4+ days/week external to Tandem actually having, and is rest duration even the right lever for it? Strip the assumption baked into the ask. EPIC-11's Expected Behavior treats "add seconds to rest" as if it were self-evidently how a system responds to accumulated fatigue. It isn't. Fatigue from external load shows up as reduced capacity to complete the SAME work (fewer reps at a given load, RPE creep) — that's a volume/intensity signal, not a between-set-interval signal. Nothing in §3 keys rest to anything but goal. Nothing in §6 keys anything to training frequency. The premise that "more outside training → more rest" is folk physiology dressed as a formula, and D23's own history (dead REST_SECONDS table) is a documented pattern of exactly this move: invent a plausible-sounding modifier table, ship it uncited, discover later it's either dead or wrong. So reject option (a) outright — not as a compromise, as a category error. You'd be adding a second axis to a system D23 just spent an entire invariant collapsing to ONE axis (goal). Option (c) is the only one asking the right question: what does external load actually predict, and what's the correctly-owned response? That's not a rest question — it's a fresh research task for volume/session-density adjustment, run through exercise-science-research from scratch, not retrofitted onto D23's rest channel because rest happened to be the field EPIC-11's author had in mind.

### The Expansionist
The narrow question — does +15s belong on rest — is the wrong frame, and answering it narrowly leaves real value on the table. I read §3 and §7 directly. §3 prescribes rest per goal only. §7 is the section that actually deals with external training-load-style signals — HRV, sleep, velocity-loss readiness — and every single lever it names is volume or intensity: "reduce volume/load slightly," "reduce volume + intensity," shift rep range, add/cut sets. Rest duration appears nowhere in §7's decision logic. That's not a gap to route around — it's a map to a bigger feature than the one EPIC-11 asked for. The upside here: "athlete load awareness" is the seed of a real-time biometric adaptation layer, Tandem's stated moat. §7 gives a fully-specified, citable traffic-light framework (≥95% green/90-95% orange/<90% red) for exactly this kind of external-stress signal — just pointed at the variables it was built for. Spending that signal on a fabricated 15-second rest bump is using a Ferrari engine to power a golf cart. Build EPIC-11 as a §7-compliant autoregulation channel — external load height maps to volume/intensity adjustment, not rest — and you get a differentiated feature instead of an uncited number bolted onto a table that's already over budget. Bonus: fixing the flagged build_muscle 135/150s overage while this is open costs nothing extra. Don't waste the escalation on rest — redirect it to the bigger, already-sourced opportunity.

### The Outsider
Reading this as someone with no history in your codebase, three things jump out immediately, and they all point the same direction. First: D23's own text already answers this. It says "no source keys rest to [X]" for the exact structural reason EPIC-11 would repeat — §3 prescribes rest by training goal, full stop, and it just doesn't have an axis for "how much you trained elsewhere this week." That's not ambiguous; that's the same shape of dead-table mistake D23 already named and killed once. Shipping EPIC-11 as spec'd would be re-committing the bug the invariant exists to prevent, with a different label on the number. Second, and this is the part an outsider notices that an insider might rationalize past: you're proposing to add +15 seconds on top of a value that's already measured to exceed its own cited ceiling (135/150s vs. the 90-120s band). Nobody has to decide anything about EPIC-11 to see that stacking an uncited increment onto an already-over-budget number compounds an unresolved problem instead of solving a new one. That flagged-open item should probably get fixed before anyone reasons about whether external load deserves a say in rest at all. Third: "training load should modify recovery" is a plausible-sounding sentence, which is exactly the trap this document's own prime directive warns about. Plausible isn't a source. There is no number here — not "the wrong number," none. Given both: option (b) is wrong (external load should eventually be allowed to talk to volume/rest somehow — don't slam that door), option (a) is wrong (nothing licenses +15s specifically), and (c) is the only one that ships something honest today.

### The Executor
Here's the Monday-morning read: D23's own text already answers this, and it answers it as (b). D23 isn't ambiguous about ownership — "PHASES is the sole owner of rest, and authoredRest is the only channel by which any deviation reaches the user." The audit's citation check confirms it: §3 prescribes rest per GOAL, full stop. Not per external load, not per skill level. There is no clause in D23 that says "goal, except when another signal wants in." Building a new authoredRest-compatible channel for EPIC-11 isn't extending the doctrine, it's rewriting who PHASES is. And the +15s number isn't a research gap you can go dig up — it's untethered by construction. Rest is a function of the SET you're about to do, not a function of how many sessions happened last Tuesday. No amount of searching fixes that; it's a category error. Worse: you'd be adding to a band that's already 15-30s over budget. Shipping +15 on top of an already-flagged overshoot compounds a known violation. Monday morning: reject (b), close the ticket, write it up as a Notion ruling citing D23 + the audit. Then open a SEPARATE, smaller ticket for the hybrid advisory (c) — that's a UI/copy change, no doctrine touch, ships independently, and gives Kerwin something real instead of nothing.

## Chairman synthesis

### Where the Council Agrees
All five advisors and all three peer reviews converge on the same core reading: D23 is dispositive, not ambiguous. §3 prescribes rest by goal with no external-load axis; §7's readiness framework routes every "compromised recovery" signal through volume, intensity, deload, or frequency — never rest. Option (a) is rejected by every single voice. There is also unanimous agreement that the +15s proposal is compounding, not just violating: it adds to a rest band (135/150s) that D23's own audit already flags as over its cited ceiling (90-120s).

### Where the Council Clashes
The real split is over how to dispose of (b) vs. (c). The Executor treats this as a clean (b) plus a separate (c) ticket. The Contrarian and First Principles Thinker land close to (b) with a conditional (c). The Expansionist pushes past the ownership question into a full §7 autoregulation-channel redirect — flagged by two of three peer reviews as scope creep. The Outsider's position is internally contradictory (asserts "(b) alone is wrong" while endorsing (c)-only). Net: the Executor's structure is the only formulation that neither overreaches nor hedges into self-contradiction.

### Blind Spots the Council Caught
1. No one checked whether §7's traffic-light framework is itself bound by D23's single-axis logic — the Expansionist's redirect might just relocate the same category error one table over.
2. The 135/150s overage was treated as background context, not its own live doctrine violation needing urgent, independent attention.
3. The mechanics of the proposed signal (">=4 external sessions/week") were never interrogated — how is it sourced/verified, and why 4, why session-count rather than density/type/duration?

### The Recommendation
Reject (a) outright. Rule this a (b) on the ownership question, split into two tickets: (1) close EPIC-11's rest-modification ask now, citing D23 + this ruling — no doctrine amendment needed; (2) a separate, independent ticket may carry hybrid-advisory (c), zero write path into any PHASES-owned value. Do not greenlight the Expansionist's full §7 autoregulation-channel build as part of this ruling — that needs its own scoped proposal and exercise-science-research pass if pursued later.

### The One Thing to Do First
Open and fix the 135/150s vs. 90-120s rest-band overage as its own bug ticket, independent of and prior to any EPIC-11 work. It is a confirmed, live D23 violation sitting in production right now.
