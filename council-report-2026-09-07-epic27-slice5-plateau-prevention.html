Full advisor responses

The Contrarian sequencing risk

This proposal has a dependency ordering problem it's trying to walk past: (b) can't be answered in isolation from (a), and the recommended answer to (a) makes the feature ship on top of a known-broken metric.

Finding 4 already told us direct-set counting is wrong for exactly triceps/biceps/delts. Ranking muscles as "lagging" off that same broken metric could flag delts as lagging because of a measurement blind spot, not real undertraining — then prescribe synergist volume to backfill an error nobody quantified. If the eventual answer to (b) is "count secondary volume with fractional credit," that retroactively changes what "lagging" meant in every prior block, touching the same effective-volume table D20 and Finding 4 both use — a "one rule, one home" violation waiting to happen if this epic invents its own formula independently.

Recommendation: sequencing, not scope, is the real risk — decide (b) before (a), not in parallel.

The First Principles Thinker is this even a new feature?

Why does this need a new mechanism at all, when D20 already is this mechanism? D20 softly steers toward under-trained muscles, never hard-excludes, always falls back. Slice 5 is asking D20's question at a different timescale. If D20's steering logic already has an operational definition of "under-trained," that's the one to reuse — not because it's scientifically superior, but because a second, differently-defined "lagging" concept at the block layer is exactly the "one rule, one home" violation the project's own doctrine forbids.

Separately: (b) is being smuggled in but isn't required by what Kerwin asked for. Supporting-lift selection is solvable entirely off muscle_secondary tags without ever crediting a fractional set value toward anyone's weekly total. Resolving Finding 4 is a separate, 7-week-stale decision with its own blast radius across every goal's floor math. Bolting it on because the data is adjacent is scope creep dressed as thoroughness.

Real question: is this a new feature, or is it D20 asked to run once more, later, at a bigger grain?

The Expansionist this is the moat, lean in

This isn't a rebalancing algorithm, it's the first mechanism where the app visibly, provably gets smarter about the user specifically, at a natural narrative beat (week 13). Ship the comparison as a visible artifact: "your chest grew 40% slower than your shoulders this block, here's your rebalanced wave" is screenshot-worthy.

Use direct-set-count now — cheapest, easiest to narrate. Bank 1RM-growth-rate as v2 once multi-block history exists; that longitudinal model is the actual switching-cost moat. Whatever the ruling on Finding 4, use this as the forcing function to finally close the 7-week-stale audit rather than let it ship as a workaround. Extend D20 — "the same intelligence that picks your workout today also plans your next 12 weeks" is a stronger pitch than the feature alone.

Biggest miss in the brief: this data is the seed of a couples-competitive muscle-balance leaderboard nobody's building yet.

The Outsider name what "lagging" actually means

Item (a) isn't four options, it's a fork the ticket is dodging: sibling-relative, MEV-floor-relative, and 1RM-growth-rate measure three different things (relative underperformance, absolute deficiency, trend). A muscle can be dead last among siblings while sitting comfortably above MEV — is that "lagging"? Pick the one that matches what the product ask literally says: relative, sibling-normalized.

Item (b) is the one nobody should skip: you can't answer (a) without knowing whether secondary volume counts, because "relative to peers" needs a volume number, and Finding 4 already says the current number is wrong. Escalate that specifically — don't quietly inherit "primary-only" as a default just because it's easier to code.

On (c) vs (d): reusing D20 at block-level is the only option that doesn't invent new machinery, which matters more to me than resolving (a)/(b) with perfect precision.

The Executor build order, straight through

(a) Direct-set count vs. sibling muscles — buildable Monday with data already logged. 1RM-growth-rate needs reliable per-muscle trend tracking that doesn't exist, and Kerwin already backed off it. MEV-floor comparison adds a second dependency for no clear gain here.

(b) Don't touch Finding 4 in this slice — it's a 7-week-old open question with real design work of its own. Use muscle_primary only. Flag Finding 4 as still-open, not resolved-by-implication.

(c) Extend D20 — it already does exactly this shape. Don't reuse antagonist-superset pairing; that was built for Transform-goal supersets, wrong tool.

(d) Different layers, no conflict, but sequence matters: rebalancing decides emphasis first, D27 continuity then picks the specific lift per slot. Don't let rebalancing re-run mid-block.
