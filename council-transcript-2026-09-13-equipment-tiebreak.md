# LLM Council — Equipment-Tier Tiebreak for Isolation/Accessory Selection

**Date:** 2026-09-13 · **Cycle:** 84 (scheduled /loop) · **Escalated via:** `.claude/loop-config.md`'s `escalation.default_for_forks`, after `exercise-science-research` confirmed the canonical sources are silent on a specific ranking rule.

## The question brought to the council

`programs.js`'s `bank()`/`select()` candidate sort for isolation/accessory generator slots was purely alphabetical by exercise name, with zero equipment-quality tiebreak. At the full_gym equipment tier, a resistance-band exercise ranked exactly equal to a cable/machine/barbell exercise targeting the same muscle group, and bands frequently won alphabetically. Measured impact (original TEST-stage sweep): 98 of 179 `EXERCISE_BANK` entries never selected across a 2,880-persona sweep, and full_gym-tier programs rendered band/bodyweight-heavy accessory blocks despite cable/machine/barbell equivalents existing and being tagged reachable for the same muscle (BUG-108, BUG-94).

Research already done (`exercise-science-research` skill, same cycle): Exercise Science Schema v0.5's `emg_mvc_pct` idea is unbuilt; Programming Architecture Reference states no general equipment-hierarchy rule and never mentions bands; `research-report (8).pdf` §4 explicitly self-flags equipment-tier equivalencies as an acknowledged research gap, giving only a qualitative lean (free weights preferred for strength/power; machines "acceptable" for isolation/hypertrophy) and never comparing bands to anything; external corroboration (NSCA, PMC6358948) supports a real but unquantified concern about band load-consistency, not a stimulus-superiority claim.

Question for the council: should the generator add an equipment-quality tiebreak at the full_gym tier, ship it as an engineering default (like existing UNSOURCED-but-flagged constants D25/D28) or wait for a citation, scope it to full_gym only or general, and does it need a doctrine invariant?

## Advisor responses (anonymized order A–E during peer review; de-anonymized here)

**The Executor:** Ship as engineering default (same class as D28/D25). Coarse 3-tier equipment-class rank, tiebreak only when tags/oneRmFactor already tied. Wire generally (mechanism not instance) since `bank()`/`select()` are shared across tiers. NOT a doctrine invariant — a persona-sweep dead-inventory regression check is the right-weight gate instead.

**The Contrarian:** The 98/179 dead-inventory number doesn't discriminate between candidate fixes — don't cite it as evidence for *this specific* fix. The proposed hierarchy risks a citation-misuse trap: the real evidence (NSCA, PMC6358948) is about band load-*consistency*, not stimulus-quality superiority; labeling this "equipment-quality" overclaims what the sources say. Isolation work is exactly where the one qualitative lean that exists says machines are merely "acceptable" — the research actually undercuts urgency, not supports it. Flagged: has anyone checked outcome data, or is this a taste preference wearing a doctrine costume?

**The First Principles Thinker:** Alphabetical-by-name was never a designed policy — it's what `.sort()` does with no comparator. This is a null hypothesis nobody chose, not a judgment call standing in for a missing number (unlike D25/D28). Removing it needs no citation-permission. Reframe: ship the smallest change that removes an arbitrary artifact without inventing fake precision — a coarse ordinal rank, not a numeric score.

**The Expansionist:** 55% dead inventory is the product silently failing its own "not static, not random" moat claim to the exact users (full_gym, most-equipped) who should see the deepest programs. Ship now; wire generally with a per-tier equipment-availability list; make it a doctrine invariant ("generator must reach every reachable-tagged exercise across a sweep"). Also floated bundling in rotation/variety enhancements while touching the sort function.

**The Outsider:** A resistance band and a cable machine "tying" and the tiebreak being alphabetical string sort is a coin flip disguised as neutrality — not a case of "the science is silent so we need a philosophy." The current behavior is *also* an unproven, unowned default. If a full_gym user selected "full_gym," why does the app hand them bands anyway? That mismatch would confuse a first-time user with no science background required.

## Peer review (5 reviewers, each anonymized to the other four)

Convergent findings, independently reached by all five reviewers:
- **Strongest response:** the Executor (3/5) and the First Principles Thinker (2/5) — both gave a concrete, correctly-scoped, shippable answer; the Contrarian's citation-integrity point was rated important but not itself a "ship the fix" answer.
- **Biggest blind spot, unanimous 5/5:** the Expansionist's proposal to bundle rotation/variety enhancements into this fix "since you're already touching the sort function" — flagged by every reviewer as scope creep that violates the project's own change-discipline, and as proposing a doctrine invariant without engaging the Contrarian's naming objection.
- **What all five advisors missed, independently caught in peer review:** (1) none of them named that this is a program-logic/generator-selection change requiring the `exercise-science-research` skill invocation and a should/could/did audit before shipping — even for an engineering default, not just a cited rule; (2) none flagged that the comparator should reuse `EXERCISE_BANK`'s *already-existing* `equipment` field rather than inventing anything new (one rule, one home); (3) none addressed whether the current tag data already encodes enough to drive this without a new field.

## Chairman's verdict

**Where the council agrees:** alphabetical sort was an unowned, undesigned default — not a judgment call requiring a citation to remove. Ship a comparator now. Use a coarse (3-class) equipment-availability rank built from the EXISTING `equipment` field. Apply it ONLY as a last-resort tiebreak, after every existing tiebreak (D20 recency, oneRmFactor), before the final alphabetical fallback. Scope it generally (both known duplicate copies of the sort — `select()` in `getSingleDay`, `bank()` in `buildDynamicProgram`) per "fix the mechanism, not the instance" — `tierOk()` already makes it a no-op at hotel_gym/home tiers where cable/machine/barbell aren't tier-legal. Do NOT promote to `scripts/doctrine.mjs` — this is inventory-utilization plumbing, not program-science correctness.

**Where the council clashes, unresolved by majority vote alone:** the Contrarian's objection that labeling this "equipment-quality" overclaims a stimulus-superiority conclusion the sources don't support. This is not outvoted — it is *correct on the facts* (the only real citations are about load-consistency, not muscle-stimulus quality) and is adopted as a binding naming/framing constraint on the implementation, not merely one voice among five.

**Blind spots the council caught in itself, via peer review:** every advisor skipped the actual CLAUDE.md-mandated procedure (should/could/did audit, honest citation of what the sources do and don't say) in favor of debating content; the fix must still produce that audit even though it's shipping as an engineering default, not a cited rule.

**The recommendation:** Ship the tiebreak as `EQUIPMENT_AVAILABILITY_RANK` / `equipmentAvailabilityRank()`, named and commented as an inventory-reachability engineering default (never "equipment-quality" or a stimulus claim), wired into both known duplicate sort sites, gated only as a last-resort tiebreak, not promoted to doctrine. Reject the Expansionist's scope-creep addendum outright.

**The one thing to do first:** write the should/could/did audit into the code comment honestly (including the Contrarian's naming objection as a rejected COULD), implement the minimal comparator, and re-run the full gate suite (validate:programs, validate:personas, verify, program-snapshot --update) to confirm the change is coherent before shipping.

## Post-verdict correction (found during this same cycle's own Fix/Verify, before ship)

Implementing the verdict literally (a tiebreak scoped to fire only between candidates sharing a primary muscle tag, to protect BUG-101's freshly-wired `band-external-rotation` slot from being crowded out) turned out to be **non-transitive** — a pairwise "do these two share a tag" gate is not guaranteed transitive across a pool of 3+ candidates, which makes `Array.prototype.sort`'s ordering implementation-defined per spec. This is a genuine correctness defect independent of the council's question, caught by directly running the generator and observing sort-order-dependent, non-reproducible-by-hand results. It was reverted in favor of the plain, ungated, transitive comparator the council actually recommended (`equipmentAvailabilityRank(a) - equipmentAvailabilityRank(b)` alone).

**Known, accepted consequence:** this means BUG-101's `band-external-rotation` no longer wins its shared `FOCUS_SLOTS.pull` slot at full_gym/hotel_gym tier (a same/cross-tag cable or dumbbell candidate now wins on the equipment tiebreak, or on the same alphabetical rule that always governed ties outside it). It remains structurally reachable (`scripts/reachability-smoke.mjs`: 179/179) and is selected at the home tier — which is BUG-101's own literal Expected Behavior ("selectable by at least one generator slot"), not a "wins every configuration" guarantee. The earlier, stronger empirical claim in BUG-101's Notion evidence (100% of sampled configs) is corrected rather than left standing, since it predates this second fix and is no longer accurate.

**Independent Verify-stage finding on the aggregate metric (not assumed from this transcript):** a fresh sweep of `getProgram()` across a full rotation range (weeks 1–24, phases 0–3) found the equipment-tiebreak fix is real and produces a genuine, measured improvement (61→37 never-selected bank entries in that sweep methodology, a ~39% reduction) — but does not fully resolve BUG-108's aggregate metric. The residual traces to a separate, pre-existing defect: `pick()`'s rotation/phase indexing only takes ~4 distinct values regardless of real program length, so large same-muscle candidate pools (e.g. a 16-member bicep isolation pool) only ever expose their top ~4 sorted ranks. This is out of scope for the tiebreak fix and is recommended as a new, separately-scoped story rather than silently claimed resolved here.
