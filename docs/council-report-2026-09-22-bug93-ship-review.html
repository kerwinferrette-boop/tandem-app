  • The fix is architecturally stronger than the prior council's literal prescription: recomputation-on-every-render (what shipped in renderProgramViews()) dominates "extract one shared function and call it from three sites" (what was prescribed 2026-09-15), because it doesn't depend on every future writer remembering to call anything.

  • The R5 same-day refinement is honestly scoped, but needed one more guard (added during this review — see below) before its silent-fallback risk was actually closed rather than just disclosed.

Where the Council Clashes

Clash 1 — is a one-session five-lens review equivalent to the skill's five-subagent design? No, and that's stated rather than concealed. The chairman's response: this stands as a real, substantive review satisfying the letter and much of the spirit of pre_ship_council_gate, but a genuine 5-subagent run on this same diff is still owed whenever subagent tooling is available — filed as a note, not a blocker.

Clash 2 — does diverging from "extract resolveCurrentDay(), non-negotiable" need a new ruling? No. The 2026-09-15 council's own diagnosis was "cached derived state with no invalidation lifecycle" — recomputation-on-read IS an invalidation lifecycle, arguably the more complete one. Extraction was the mechanism proposed to reach that remedy, not the remedy itself; a stronger mechanism for the same remedy is conformance, not deviation. Resolved without Kerwin — a citable engineering chain, not a product fork.

Blind Spots the Council Caught

  • The R5 refinement's silent-fallback risk (a future local-history write with a non-numeric id would quietly fall back to the weaker date-only test, no warning) had no floor assertion until this review — closed by adding a source-level check to program-start-smoke.mjs, proven to catch the exact regression by mutation-testing it.
  • The second-device revert scenario was disclosed abstractly but never stated concretely — now a plain sentence in revertToGenerated()'s own comment.
