Council — D32 and weight direction

Tandem · 2026-11-17 · 5 advisors, 3 peer reviewers

The question

Should doctrine rule D32 be amended so weight direction (gain vs lose) changes the generated program? The engine used the absolute difference between current and target weight, so a man on Burn Fat who wanted to gain 25 lb was treated as having 25 lb to lose.

Verdict

  1. Fix the discarded direction and correct D32's wording with it: the number is pounds to lose, and a gain target is zero.
  2. Do not add a cardio rule for gainers. The only number available is about training interference, not about gaining, and it does not show harm above two sessions a week.
  3. Do not build the 10–20 lb and under-10 lb tiers. They are uncited thresholds from a superseded epic.
  4. Do not tie load progression to weight direction. No source supports it; the one study found strength gains preserved in a deficit.
  5. Tell the user when a goal and a target weight contradict each other (Burn Fat with a higher target). Product decision.

First step: ship the direction fix with its gate assertion.

What peer review added

  • "Minimal interference at two sessions a week" does not show harm above two, so it cannot justify a cap for anyone.
  • The 20 lb threshold and the men-only scope in D32 are themselves unsourced.
  • The number moves as body weight changes, so a user crossing 20 lb mid-block changes program; when it is evaluated is unspecified.
  • Nobody had checked who is affected. Checked afterwards: no stored account is on Burn Fat and none has a target above its current weight.
Advisor positions

Contrarian. Target weight is an aspiration, not a measured energy balance. The doctrine literally says absolute value, so changing it is an amendment. No gainer rule; the real question is whether the every-day finisher is supported for anyone.

First Principles. Direction is an energy-balance intent the engine has no variable for; D32 should not be its home. Fix the sign in scope. A cap would belong to the goal.

Expansionist. Make direction a first-class input, cap gainers at two finishers a week, keep a table with pending rows, wire direction into emphasis and explanations.

Outsider. A user who types a higher target believes they said "I want to gain". Fix the behaviour and the wording. Cap at two. Do not build tiers nobody could explain.

Executor. One small signed fix with a gate assertion; flag everything else; open the every-day finisher as its own question.

Peer review

Two of three reviewers rated the Executor strongest and one the First Principles response; all three named the Expansionist as having the biggest blind spot (a direction-keyed cap the source does not support, and pending rows that give uncited thresholds a home).
