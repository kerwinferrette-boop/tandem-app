LLM Council · 2026-10-06

Should the cited 6-set-per-exercise cap become a D-invariant?

BUG-231: the MEV raise loop satisfied a muscle's weekly volume floor by piling sets onto one exercise — 7×12 Decline Barbell Press, up to 10–11 sets of one lift on 2-day splits. Research produced a 6-set-per-exercise-per-session ceiling from three primary sources (ACSM 2009, Krieger 2010, Hackett 2018). The fix is shipped and gated.

But D33 — already ACTIVE — says in its own text that the per-exercise cap is "a consequence, not a separate claim." New D-number, D33 clause, or not doctrine at all? Which tier? And is a rule with 700 standing exceptions an invariant at all?

Branch bug231-per-exercise-cap @ 81a01ec · 5 advisors → 5 anonymized peer reviews → chairman synthesis · Full transcript: council-transcript-2026-10-06-bug231-per-exercise-cap-promotion.md

Verdict in one line

Don't promote "6." Promote the allocation rule instead — and amend D33's sentence, which is now false under every option including "do nothing."

The research establishes "unknown above 6," not "6 is a ceiling." An absence-of-evidence claim can't be violated, so it can't be an invariant. What the fix actually changed is how the engine allocates a deficit — and that is a closed, checkable claim with zero exceptions by construction.

Where the Council Agrees

  1. "6" is not a bound. Four of five reached this independently: ACSM's 3–6 is a range where benefit was observed; Krieger's top bin stops at 6 because that's how the literature clustered, so silence above 6 is a measurement-grid artifact; Hackett is an n=12 pilot whose lean-mass outcome moved in neither arm. The Contrarian's compression: "Stack three nulls and you still have a null."
  2. An absence-of-evidence claim cannot be an invariant. Invariants assert something violable. "Nobody has measured past here" isn't.
  3. The 34 cells are debt, not law. All 700 remaining over-ceiling lifts share one mechanism — no legal additional lift exists at that tier. That's a bank-coverage defect already named in D33's own known gap and blocked on muscle_tag_rescope.
  4. The buried finding outranks the cap. Three advisors raised it unprompted: D6b's MEV floor was green on main partly because of this bug. A cited floor that was only green by accident is a bigger integrity problem than an uncited plateau.
  5. SAFETY is off the table. Nothing cited shows harm above 6 — SAFETY would be, in the Executor's phrase, "a lie you'd have to defend."

Where the Council Clashes

1. Promote nothing vs. promote something else. Contrarian and Outsider both land on "keep the gate, promote nothing." The Outsider's reason is the sharpest thing anyone said:

"A ceiling you deliberately route around 700 times is a default with a tiebreak. Calling it an invariant and then shipping 34 standing exceptions teaches everyone reading /DOCTRINE.md that D-numbers are negotiable — which is the one property your gate depends on not being true."

Against them, Executor wants durability ("#44 is a standalone script someone will 'simplify' in six months, whereas a D-row has a Notion page explaining why 6") and Expansionist wants leverage. Both sides are weighing real costs. First Principles is the only advisor who refuses the trade — and that escape is what every reviewer picked as strongest.

2. Register the OVERRIDE_KEYS entry now? Expansionist calls a keyless SCIENCE_DEFAULT "a dead letter" and the seam a future coach mode needs. Executor calls it speculative work. Four of five reviewers sided against the Expansionist on a specific technical ground: the project's own tier vocabulary says a keyless SCIENCE_DEFAULT authorizes no deviation at all — the tier records confidence, not permission. "Dead letter" misreads the vocabulary rather than finding a gap in it.

3. New D-number vs. D33 clause. Executor: don't braid 6 to a PROVISIONAL 11 revisit-dated 2027-03-23 — you'll want to move 11 without touching 6. Contrarian: a new D-number asserts a new claim, and you don't have one. Both are right about their own concern, which is the signal that the thing being numbered is wrong, not the numbering.

Blind Spots the Council Caught

  1. D33's sentence is now false — and must be amended under EVERY option, including "do nothing." Four of five reviewers caught this; no advisor did. D33 affirmatively claims ownership: "The per-exercise cap is a consequence, not a separate claim." Written when Remmert was the only source; now contradicted by three primary sources with a different unit, bound and authors. Promoting a new D-number while leaving it standing ships two ACTIVE invariants disputing ownership of one rule — a one-rule-one-home violation. Leaving the rule in the gate doesn't help; the sentence is still false.
  2. The most attractive answer was rejected unanimously. All five reviewers named the Expansionist the biggest blind spot. Reviewer 4: "pure upside narration… never tests whether the bound exists." Reviewer 3: "architecture astronomy built atop a bound nobody has shown exists above 6." An unverified premise in strategic language is still an unverified premise.
  3. The pass-2 escape hatch is load-bearing and invisible to the gate. One reviewer asked whether an uncapped second pass could push a muscle past D33's ~11.
    4. Checked against the code rather than reasoned about: fits() (programs.js:3474) applies the SESSION_MUSCLE_CEILING term unconditionally — the capped flag gates only the per-exercise line. Pass 2 cannot violate D33. The reviewer's narrower framing stands: this is a gap in what the gate asserts versus what the code does.
  4. One rule, one home — partly a misread, worth stating precisely. Three reviewers assumed 6 is duplicated in engine and gate. It isn't: PER_EXERCISE_SET_CEILING is declared once (programs.js:3393) and the gate vm-extracts it live; the only second literal is the pin CAP === 6, which is the gate doing its job. But the instinct about naming is right — a bare const beside the raise loop is a weaker home than a named table, and the number carries no revisit date the way D33's 11 does.

The Recommendation

  1. Promote First Principles' claim, not mine. No exercise-science number, zero exceptions by construction:
    The engine must never satisfy a weekly volume floor by adding sets to an existing exercise when a legal additional exercise exists.
    When no legal lift exists the antecedent is false and the rule is silent — exactly where the 700 cases live. This is also the rule D33's own known gap already names in prose and never made executable.
  2. New D-number (D35), not a D33 clause. D33's 11 is PROVISIONAL and revisit-dated; you'll want to move it without touching this. The Contrarian's objection ("asserts a new claim you don't have") is answered by item 1 — the claim is now about the allocator, which is new and is ours. Matches the D34-over-D24-clause precedent.
  3. SCIENCE_DEFAULT. No OVERRIDE_KEYS entry. SAFETY is indefensible; a key is speculative permission for a deviation nobody requested, and a keyless SCIENCE_DEFAULT is already fully binding today.
  4. Amend D33's ownership sentence so it cedes the per-exercise unit to D35 and keeps D33 to the per-muscle unit. Notion mirror first, then /DOCTRINE.md and doctrine.mjs in one commit.
  5. Keep "6" as a parameter; bolt on the Executor's ratchet. The number stays in code with its citations, its honest limits, and — new — a revisit date, the way D33's 11 carries one. Then commit the 34 forced cells to a fixture and assert the live set is ≤ 34 and a subset. A floating exception count becomes a monotonic burn-down; a new bank-coverage gap goes red the day it appears; and the stalled muscle_tag_rescope item finally has a finish line.

File separately, not part of the promotion: (a) the buried finding — whether D6b's MEV gate was ever mutation-tested against stacked allocation (an open SC-38/SC-39 question three advisors argued deserves an entry more than the cap does); (b) the Contrarian's "what audits the forced classifier?" — check #44's forced-ness computation shares inputs with the engine, and "it was mutation-tested" should be verified assertion-by-assertion, not asserted.

The One Thing to Do First

Amend D33's sentence in Notion. Not the new D-number, not the fixture, not the push.

That sentence — "the per-exercise cap is a consequence, not a separate claim" — is false as of this research, and false under every option this council considered, including "promote nothing." It's the only unconditionally required item, it's the Notion-first step that gates the /DOCTRINE.md and doctrine.mjs edits, and until it's fixed the doctrine contains a standing claim the sources contradict. Everything else follows from it.

Advisor responses in full

The Contrarian promote nothing · "stack three nulls and you still have a null"

Three citations, zero of them bound 6. ACSM's 3–6 is a recommended range for advanced trainees, not a ceiling — ranges describe where benefit was observed, they do not forbid the outside. Krieger measured nothing above 6 sets; that is absence of evidence, and you are reading it as evidence of absence. And Hackett could not detect its own primary outcome — lean mass moved in neither arm of an n=12 pilot with uncontrolled diet — so it cannot license a bound on anything. Stack three nulls and you still have a null.

The 700 exceptions are unfalsifiable as written. You say every one is "structurally forced" and computed independently. What audits the forced classifier? If the classifier shares an assumption with the engine, the gate rubber-stamps whatever the engine does and reports zero violations forever.

And the buried finding is the real one here, not the cap: D6b's MEV floor was green on main partly BECAUSE of this stacking. The defect is exercise selection breadth, not set counting. Capping sets caps the symptom and leaves the mechanism.

Answers: (1) keep check #44, promote nothing. (2) If forced to promote, a D33 clause — a new D-number asserts a new claim and you do not have one. (3) A SCIENCE_DEFAULT with no OVERRIDE_KEYS entry is permissionless theater. (4) No. It's a volume budget heuristic. Name it that.

The First Principles Thinker strongest, 5/5 reviewers · promote the allocation rule

You are asking "what tier and what number," which presumes the rule you've written is the rule you actually discovered. It isn't.

Strip it back. What did the evidence establish? Not "6 is a ceiling." It established that nobody has measured a benefit above 6 sets of one exercise in one session. That is a statement about the literature's coverage, not about muscle. An invariant whose content is absence of evidence is a category error — invariants assert what must be true, and "we haven't looked past here" can't be violated.

Now look at what the fix actually does. It doesn't enforce 6. It changes how the engine ALLOCATES a volume deficit: spread across exercises before stacking on one. And the 700 "exceptions" all share exactly one mechanism — no legal additional lift exists for that muscle at that tier. That's not 34 carve-outs from a law. That's a bank-coverage and selection-breadth defect, enumerated. Thirty-four cells of technical debt.

So promote the rule D33 already names as its own gap, and state it without any exercise-science number: the engine must never satisfy a weekly volume floor by adding sets to an existing exercise when a legal additional exercise exists. That is a closed, checkable claim about engine behavior. Zero exceptions by construction — when no legal lift exists the condition is simply false. No plateau hand-waving required.

"6" then becomes what it is: a parameter of the allocator, versioned next to REP_BANDS, not a law.

The Expansionist biggest blind spot, 5/5 reviewers · promote + register the key

The 700 forced exceptions are the most valuable output of this work, and you're treating them as an embarrassment. Promote it: new D-number, SCIENCE_DEFAULT, and register the OVERRIDE_KEYS entry now. A SCIENCE_DEFAULT with no registered key is a dead letter — and the key is exactly the legal seam you will need for hand-authored programming and a future coach mode.

Three upsides nobody else is looking at. First, this cap is a diagnostic instrument, not just a ceiling: the counterfactual you already ran (flat cap ⇒ 46 muscle-weeks below MEV) is a new genre of gate — invariants that audit other invariants by counterfactual. Point that technique at D28 and D8 next. Second, the 34 cells are not debt, they're a prioritized worklist for the muscle-tag vocabulary gap D33 flagged in prose and never scoped. Fixing them adds real exercise variety — a visible product win — and self-liquidates the exceptions. That makes the exception count a roadmap metric. Third, and biggest: "an unmeasured plateau yields to a measured floor" is the first explicit precedence relation between two cited bounds in this doctrine. Formalize it as an ordering, not a footnote. A machine-checkable precedence lattice over cited bounds is a genuinely defensible moat artifact — nobody else building fitness software has one. This is also the doctrine's first plateau-tier bound; that is the template for every "no measured benefit above X" finding you will ever cite.

An invariant with located, counted, mechanically-derived exceptions is stronger law than one with none — because it knows where it doesn't apply.

The Outsider promote nothing · "a default with a tiebreak"

"6" is an artifact of how the studies were binned, not a property of muscles. Krieger's top bin was 4–6 because that's how the literature clustered; had the bin been 4–8, you'd be promoting D-whatever at 8 with equal confidence. The honest number isn't 6, it's "unknown above 6."

On Hackett: lean mass moved in neither arm. If the study couldn't detect growth in the group that DID grow — and people training 12 weeks grow — that's a failed instrument, not a null result. A reader with no stake in this will spot that immediately, and once they do, every number downstream of it looks chosen rather than found.

Here's what reads strangest from outside. The thing you're describing isn't a ceiling. A ceiling you deliberately route around 700 times is a default with a tiebreak. Calling it an invariant and then shipping 34 standing exceptions teaches everyone reading /DOCTRINE.md that D-numbers are negotiable — which is the one property your gate depends on not being true. And the buried finding deserves its own entry far more than this does: D6b was passing partly because of the bug. A cited floor that was only green by accident is a bigger integrity problem than an uncited plateau.
