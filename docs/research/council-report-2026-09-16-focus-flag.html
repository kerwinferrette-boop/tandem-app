LLM Council Verdict

"Focus this lift" engine mechanism — Tandem · 2026-09-16

The Question

Two things the "Focus this lift" feature cannot ship without, that no canonical Tandem source (DOCTRINE.md, Notion, repo research) answers:

  1. Simultaneous-focus cap — how many muscle groups can a user flag "focus" at once?
  2. Mechanism — does focusing ADD volume on top of the program, or REDISTRIBUTE it away from other muscles?

Where the Council Agrees

The only currently-gated volume bound in Tandem is the MEV floor (D6b) — MAV/MRV are documented but ungated, so pure addition has no ceiling to push against. Redistribution is the only mechanism consistent with the system as it exists today — not a preference, a constraint. All 5 peer reviewers independently named the First Principles Thinker's response strongest, and the Executor's plan as the one that actually violates the Prime Directive.

Where the Council Clashes

Not mechanism — posture. Executor wants to ship now assuming MEV-as-maintenance is "close enough"; Contrarian calls that the load-bearing unverified risk in the whole feature. Expansionist wants redistribution surfaced as a differentiated, transparent trust feature; Outsider warns that invisible redistribution leaves users (and Kerwin) unable to explain why other lifts got lighter.

Blind Spots the Council Caught

Feature-level: Executor's plan silently treats a growth-minimum landmark (MEV) as a validated maintenance-dose target — "exactly the confident fabrication CLAUDE.md's prime directive forbids," per multiple reviewers.
Process-level (caught only in peer review): No advisor, and no version of this question's framing, actually did a targeted check of Notion's Exercise Science Schema v0.5 or Periodization spec for a maintenance-volume or specialization-block concept. Every argument here rests on a corroboration-only blog + code reading + first-principles biology. This verdict is not yet doctrine-ready on process grounds alone.

The Recommendation

Ship the engine mechanism as redistribution, not addition, with the cap treated as emergent: however many muscles can be dropped to their MEV floor while still leaving enough weekly capacity to meaningfully elevate the focused muscle(s). Do not hardcode a cap constant. Do not reuse VOLUME_LANDMARKS's MEV value as a maintenance target without a citation — treat it as an open variable pending the Notion check. If a source is found, name the new table MAINTENANCE_LANDMARKS rather than overloading MEV silently. Wire into computeMuscleWeeklyVolume(); add the missing D20/D27-equivalent tiebreak inside buildDynamicProgram()'s bank(). Avoid the FOCUS_SLOTS naming collision. Surface redistribution transparently in the UI — this can ship in the same PR without waiting on the doctrine question.

The One Thing to Do First

Read Exercise Science Schema v0.5 and the Periodization spec specifically for a maintenance-volume or specialization-block concept — not a general sweep, a targeted search by name. If found, cite it and set the dose accordingly. If confirmed silent, that silence is itself the artifact to record, and the next move is another llm-council round to rule on the MAINTENANCE_LANDMARKS numeric default — not a guess, not MEV-reuse.
Full Advisor Responses
The Contrarian
The feature has a fatal unverified parameter — nothing validates that Tandem's MEV floor can double as a "maintenance dose" for de-prioritized muscles. MEV is a growth minimum, not a validated maintenance minimum; treating them as interchangeable is an unstated assumption at the center of the whole feature. Also flagged safety/incoherence risk from an unbounded simultaneous-focus count.
The First Principles Thinker (strongest)
Reframed both sub-questions as one physical-constraint argument: fixed weekly training capacity + the only currently-gated bound (MEV floor) means redistribution is the only internally coherent mechanism — pure addition has no ceiling to push against (MAV/MRV ungated) and would silently blow past volume landmarks or session length. Under redistribution, the "cap" isn't an independent number to invent — it's emergent: however many muscles can be dropped to their MEV floor while still leaving enough weekly capacity to elevate the focused muscle(s) meaningfully.
The Expansionist
Framed "Focus this lift" as competitive differentiation for Tandem's science-backed-adaptive-program moat — a well-executed, transparently-shown redistribution ("we're trimming X to grow Y this block") could become a trust-building feature, not just a filter toggle. Argued not to under-scope UI ambition even with a conservative engine mechanism.
The Outsider
No Tandem context. Flagged that "Focus this lift" as a product name collides with the pre-existing, unrelated FOCUS_SLOTS table — a real trap for a future engineer or Kerwin reading a diff. Also raised a naive-user question: if redistribution happens invisibly, what explains to the user why their other lifts got lighter — a UX/communication requirement, not just an engine one.
The Executor (biggest blind spot)
Ship fast — hardcode FOCUS_CAP = 2, drop non-focused muscles to their existing VOLUME_LANDMARKS MEV value as the "maintenance dose," ship Monday. Treated MEV as good enough to double as maintenance without further justification.
